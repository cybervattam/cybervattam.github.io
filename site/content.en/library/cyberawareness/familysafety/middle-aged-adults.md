Middle-aged adults often manage significant financial responsibilities, including home loans, children's education, retirement investments, and workplace procurement. Cybercriminals frequently target this age group with sophisticated blended threats that cross the boundary between personal life and professional obligations.

## Common Threats

- **Investment and Stock/Crypto Schemes**: Sophisticated fraudulent investment websites, false trading advisers, and pre-IPO stock opportunities targeting retirement portfolios and savings. Learn more about [Investment Scams](/library/cyberawareness/topscamsfrauds/investment-scams/).
- **Online Shopping and Marketplace Fraud**: Bogus sellers, counterfeit merchant storefronts, and overpayment scams when selling personal items or vehicles online. Review [Online Sales Scams](/library/cyberawareness/topscamsfrauds/online-sales-scams/) and [Credit Card Fraud](/library/cyberawareness/topscamsfrauds/credit-card-fraud/).
- **Phishing and Vishing in Professional Contexts**: Targeted emails and phone calls that mimic banks, utility companies, tax departments, or suppliers requesting credential verification. See [Phishing](/library/cyberawareness/topscamsfrauds/phishing/), [Scam Calls and Spam Calls](/library/cyberawareness/topscamsfrauds/scam-calls-and-spam-calls/), and [Email Scams](/library/cyberawareness/topscamsfrauds/email-scams/).
- **Business Email Compromise and Intercepted Invoices**: Hacked or spoofed supplier emails altering banking details for large payments, home settlements, or contractors. Explore [Business Email Compromise](/library/cyberawareness/topscamsfrauds/email-compromise-scams/).
- **Account Takeovers and Identity Fraud**: Theft of personal details used to apply for credit cards, take out loans, or hijack telecommunication accounts. See [Identity Theft and Identity Fraud](/library/cyberawareness/topscamsfrauds/identity-theft-fraud/) and [Phone Porting and SIM Swap Scams](/library/cyberawareness/topscamsfrauds/phone-porting-scams/).
- **Hybrid Technical Attacks**: QR code traps, malicious attachments, and remote software prompts disguised as technical support or document readers. Explore [Remote Access Scams](/library/cyberawareness/topscamsfrauds/remote-access-scams/), [Quishing and QR Code Scams](/library/cyberawareness/topscamsfrauds/quishing/), and [Ransomware](/library/cyberawareness/topscamsfrauds/ransomware/).

## Avoidance Steps

- **Verify Financial Advisers and Investments**: Cross-reference any financial adviser, broker, or investment entity with official government registers before committing funds.
- **Navigate Directly to Services**: Type official URLs directly into your browser or use verified mobile apps rather than following links in emails or SMS notifications.
- **Confirm Invoices and Bank Detail Changes**: Always call the vendor, builder, or solicitor on an independently verified telephone number before acting on updated bank account numbers.
- **Monitor Accounts and Review Credit Reports**: Regularly examine bank and credit card statements, enable transaction alerts, and check credit scores for unexpected inquiries.
- **Promote Household Cyber Hygiene**: Ensure devices across the home are kept updated and discuss common scam tactics with children, teens, and aging parents.

## Universal Red Flags

- Last-minute changes to payment instructions or bank accounts for high-value transactions.
- Urgent claims of outstanding tax penalties, toll road fines, or package delivery failures requiring immediate payment.
- High-pressure sales tactics that attempt to bypass your usual verification or consultation processes.
- Requests to install remote diagnostic or desktop-sharing software to resolve an alleged problem.
