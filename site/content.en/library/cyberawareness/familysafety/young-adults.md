Young adults, including Gen Z and younger Millennials, are digital natives who conduct the majority of their shopping, banking, employment searches, and social interactions online. Despite their digital fluency, surveys consistently show younger adults report frequent financial losses to social-media-originated scams, employment fraud, and speculative investment schemes.

## Common Threats

- **Social-Media-Originated Scams**: Deceptive advertisements, sponsored influencer promotions, and fake brand giveaways designed to collect payment credentials or personal data. Learn more about [Social Media Scams](/library/cyberawareness/topscamsfrauds/social-media-scams/), [Online Sales Scams](/library/cyberawareness/topscamsfrauds/online-sales-scams/), and [Unexpected Money Scams](/library/cyberawareness/topscamsfrauds/unexpected-money-scams/).
- **Speculative Investment and Crypto Schemes**: High-yield investment programs, fake trading bots, and social-media tips promising unrealistic returns with zero risk. Read about [Investment Scams](/library/cyberawareness/topscamsfrauds/investment-scams/).
- **Job and Employment Scams**: Work-from-home offers, task scams, and fake recruitment where applicants are asked to pay upfront onboarding fees, buy equipment, or process fraudulent payments. Review [Job Scams](/library/cyberawareness/topscamsfrauds/job-scams/).
- **Deepfake Videos and Audio Cloning**: Artificial intelligence tools used to generate synthetic videos or clone voices of colleagues, executives, or friends asking for urgent assistance. Learn about [AI-Powered Scams](/library/cyberawareness/topscamsfrauds/artificial-intelligence-scams/) and [Spoof Calls and Fake Caller IDs](/library/cyberawareness/topscamsfrauds/spoof-calls-and-fake-calls/).
- **Peer-to-Peer Payment Fraud and Money Muling**: Solicitations to move funds through personal bank accounts, payment apps, or cryptocurrency wallets under the guise of casual work. Explore [Money Mule Scams](/library/cyberawareness/topscamsfrauds/money-mule-scams/).
- **Phishing and Account Takeovers**: Credential harvesting via fake login portals, malicious links, and SIM hijacking. See [Phishing](/library/cyberawareness/topscamsfrauds/phishing/), [Phone Porting and SIM Swap Scams](/library/cyberawareness/topscamsfrauds/phone-porting-scams/), and [Identity Theft and Identity Fraud](/library/cyberawareness/topscamsfrauds/identity-theft-fraud/).

## Avoidance Steps

- **Pause and Verify**: Stop before clicking on sponsored social media deals, urgent direct messages, or too-good-to-be-true investment offers.
- **Verify Employment Opportunities**: Thoroughly research companies through independent corporate directories; never send money for equipment, background checks, or activation.
- **Treat Cloned Media with Skepticism**: If a friend or coworker contacts you via voice or video asking for urgent money transfers, verify by calling them directly on their known, verified phone number.
- **Never Move Money for Others**: Refuse any proposal to receive and transfer funds on behalf of an employer, online acquaintance, or acquaintance. Money muling is a serious legal offense.
- **Secure Accounts with Multi-Factor Authentication**: Use dedicated authenticator apps or hardware keys instead of SMS wherever possible, and maintain unique passwords using a password manager.

## Universal Red Flags

- Fast-money guarantees or high returns with "zero risk."
- Employers who hire without an interview and require payments from you.
- Urgent requests from contacts claiming their phone is broken or demanding payments through gift cards or crypto.
- Requests to move conversations to encrypted chat apps immediately after initial contact.
